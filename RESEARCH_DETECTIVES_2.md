## 1. Detective-by-detective method cards

Each card: the method, the signature move, which Murder Board agent
it maps to, and one line the demo can say out loud.

### Sherlock Holmes — observation of the small thing
Method: the physical world keeps a memory of everything that touches
it. Worn sleeve, mud splash pattern, callus, tobacco ash, typewriter
typeface. The dog that did NOT bark proves the intruder was known.
("You see, but you do not observe.")
Signature move: read the trace before you hear the story.
Maps to: CSI (objects, ledger counts) and Analyst (the trace placed
on the timeline).
Demo line: "The file says the car was in the shop. The tow record
says it came home two days before. The trace beats the story."

### Hercule Poirot — order, method, and the grey cells
Method: sit, think, order the facts. Poirot solves most cases without
visiting the scene again: he lines up every statement, finds the one
detail out of place, and gathers everyone to walk the logic out loud.
He trusts psychology over footprints.
Signature move: the drawing-room reconstruction — every person, one
timeline, contradictions read aloud.
Maps to: Cross-Examiner and Lead Detective (the brief).
Demo line: "Poirot never chases. He lines the statements up and
waits for the one that cannot stand next to the others."

### Columbo — already in SPEC6, the one extra beat
The beat SPEC6 does not name: Columbo never accuses until the killer
has to act. He applies pressure until the murderer does something
new — moves evidence, lies a second time — and that new act is the
proof. The trap is not the evidence. It is the behaviour the
evidence forces.
Maps to: Interviewer (the next question is designed to force a
second, checkable claim).
Demo line: "One more thing: we have not checked that record yet."

### Adrian Monk — the thing that does not fit
Method: obsessive attention to order. A tilted frame, a scuff mark,
a room too clean. Monk notices what is wrong with the scene, not
what is in it. The scene was staged, and staging leaves a seam.
Signature move: "Here's what happened" — rebuild the scene from
the seam backwards.
Maps to: CSI (scene reconstruction) and Cross-Examiner (the
staged detail vs the statement).
Demo line: "The scene is too neat. Somebody tidied a murder."

### Miss Marple — the village is the database
Method: people repeat themselves. Marple solves murders by matching
a stranger to a type she has seen in her village: the same motive,
the same lie, the same cover story, in a new coat. Human nature is
the prior.
Signature move: pattern-match the person, then verify against the
record — never the other way round.
Maps to: Records (name-variant and pattern search) and Analyst
(link chart: same motive shape, different people).
Demo line: "We have seen this lie before. Now we check if this
file carries it."

### Philip Marlowe — walk the streets, follow the money
Method: Chandler's detective does not deduce from an armchair. He
knocks, he watches, he follows who got paid. The case breaks where
money changes hands, not where the alibi sounds good.
Signature move: follow the payment, the filing, the beneficiary.
Maps to: Lead Detective leads (every lead is a record pull or a
question) and Analyst links (money, work, place).
Demo line: "Follow the paperwork: who was paid, who filed, who
benefited?" (already in SPEC6 — Marlowe is why it is there.)

### Harry Bosch — the murder book, every page
Method: Bosch lives by the book. The case is the binder: every
interview, every lab slip, every photo, in order, so any detective
can pick it up cold. He re-reads the book when the case goes cold,
because the answer was usually in it on page 40.
Signature move: re-read, do not re-guess.
Maps to: First Officer (custody log, registry) — the whole build.
Demo line: "Everybody counts or nobody counts." (Bosch's line,
and the reason the board counts quotes, not hunches.)

### Mindhunter / Holden Ford — the profiler
Method: interview the killers you already have to learn the ones
you do not. Profiling predicts behaviour shape — organised vs
disorganised scene, staging vs impulse — and narrows who to look
at, never names who did it.
Signature move: the scene tells you the mind; the mind tells you
the next scene.
Maps to: Prosecutor (sufficiency: a profile is a lead, never
evidence) and Analyst (MO pattern across events).
Demo line, and the guardrail: "A profile narrows the search.
It is not an identification. The board never prints one as a
finding."

### Rust Cohle, True Detective — time is a flat circle
Method: Cohle works the pattern across years. Same ritual, same
place type, same victim shape, different decade. The killer repeats
because the killer cannot help it.
Signature move: lay the old cases next to the new one and read
the overlap.
Maps to: Records (archive trawl) and Analyst (link chart across
time).
Demo line: "One case is a crime. Three cases with the same shape
is a pattern. The board shows the pattern and stops there."

### Veronica Mars — the kid with the camera and the database
Method: a teenager with no badge solves cases with a camera, a
school directory, and public records. She photographs, she asks
around, she checks the yearbook against the story. Access beats
authority.
Signature move: the photo that contradicts the story, timestamped.
Maps to: Digital (machine witnesses: photos, timestamps) and
Records (public-record stack).
Demo line: "No badge. Just a timestamped photo and a record
that says otherwise."

### Lisbeth Salander / Mikael Blomkvist — the hacker and the reporter
Method: Salander finds what is in the systems; Blomkvist tests it
against people and paper. One half pulls the hidden record, the
other half refuses to print it until a second source carries it.
Signature move: two-source rule — nothing prints on one source.
Maps to: Digital (record pulls) + Cross-Examiner (every finding
needs two quotes, or one quote plus a record).
Demo line: "One source is a rumour. Two sources is a finding."

### Luther / the obsessive — the cost line
Method: Luther gets inside the killer's head and it costs him.
Included as the warning card: obsession finds the killer and
breaks the detective. The board is built to NOT need obsession —
the method is written down, repeatable, and any agent can run it.
Maps to: the whole runner — method in code, not in one person's
head.
Demo line: "The method is in the file, not in one detective's
gut. Anyone can pick this board up cold."

## 2. The internet detective playbook (OSINT, desk work only)

This is the second half of Del's bottleneck note: the AI cannot
contact people, so it works the public internet the way real
internet detectives already do. Every move below is desk work.
Nothing here knocks on a door.

### The moves, in order
1. **Timestamp first.** A post proves when it was posted, not when
   the thing in it happened. Screenshot time, upload time, and
   event time are three different clocks. Tag each one.
2. **Background beats caption.** Geolocation from what is behind
   the person: street sign, mural, shopfront, mountain shape,
   licence plate, bus route. The caption lies; the background
   rarely knows how.
3. **Cross-platform alias match.** Same handle, same photo, same
   phrase, same typo across Instagram, TikTok, Reddit, YouTube.
   One alias, many accounts — or two people, one name. Never merge
   on a hunch (Records rule).
4. **The livestream clock.** A livestream, a chat replay, a comment
   timestamped during the event. "In the studio all night" dies
   when the livestream shows the same person somewhere else, or
   when the chat logs the exact minute.
5. **Deleted is not gone.** A deleted story or post often survives
   as a quote, a screenshot in a reply, an archive, or somebody
   else's repost. The board records it as "deleted post, surviving
   only as a quote in X" — and ranks the pull of the original as
   the top gap. Never treat the quote as the post itself.
6. **Lyric is not evidence.** A lyric, a diss, a boast is art,
   threat, or confession — the file cannot tell which from the
   words alone. It is a lead to check against a record, never a
   finding on its own. (The Screens File already does this: the
   lyric was never promoted to evidence.)
7. **Crowd collation, judged.** A Reddit thread that collates a
   night is useful labour: timestamps gathered, links in one
   place. The board's job is to judge the collation — which items
   have sources, which are guesses, where the thread fights
   itself (wrong mural location, flagged as a fight, not a fact).
8. **The verification ladder.** Claim → corroboration → record.
   A claim with no corroboration is a gap. A claim with one
   corroboration is a lead. A claim against a record is a
   finding. Nothing climbs the ladder on vibes.

### Real cases that prove the playbook (and its limits)
- **Gabby Petito, 2021.** TikTok and Reddit mapped the couple's
  route from Instagram geotags and van photos; YouTubers Red,
  White & Bethune spotted the van in Grand Teton background
  footage while editing, shared it, and her body was found hours
  later. The crowd did the canvass in public. The lesson for the
  demo: background footage is evidence, and the crowd finds it
  faster than any single detective.
- **FBG Duck, Chicago, 2020.** Five suspects arrested; the FBI
  agent at the press conference said it plainly: the case cracked
  because the suspects bragged about it on social media and in
  drill videos. "You can go on the internet right now, you can
  see people in music videos, guns all over the place." Drill
  posts are a public record of beef, place, and time — and of
  claims that then need a record to back them.
- **Enfield, London, 2025-26.** Two 18-year-old drill rappers
  convicted of murdering Keanu Harker, 18, shot on an e-bike.
  The killing followed a row on Snapchat; prosecutors showed
  Snapchat group messages and drill videos in which gang members
  bragged about the murder. The detective's line: months of
  building a case from the digital footprint. Social media was
  the motive trail and the brag trail at once.
- **MO3 / Yella Beezy trial, Dallas, 2025.** Homicide detective
  Eric Barnes testified as an expert on drill and trap: drill is
  "representative of the violence, personal beefs, and putting
  information out about acts of violence that are going to be
  carried out or have been carried out." A song and video were
  played as evidence. The lesson: lyrics enter court as context
  for beef, not as a confession — exactly the board's rule.
- **Boston Marathon, 2013 — the warning.** Reddit sleuths
  misidentified Sunil Tripathi as a bomber and doxxed him; his
  family got threats. The crowd was almost universally wrong,
  and the wrong theories complicated the real investigation.
  This is WHY the board has quote-or-drop, innocent alternatives,
  and never names a person as a finding. The demo should say
  this out loud to the judges: the board exists because the
  crowd is fast and sometimes wrong.

### Drill-scene specifics (for the Screens File and its sequel)
- Beef is public and timestamped: diss tracks, stories, comments,
  and replies form a dated chain. A threat dated before the
  killing is motive context; a boast dated after it is a claim
  to check against the record.
- Place is in the video: block, mural, corner store, estate
  stairwell. Fans geolocate it in hours. The board treats a
  fan geolocation as UNVERIFIED until a second source carries it.
- The deleted post is the most cited item and the weakest: in
  the Screens File the 1 AM deleted story is in nobody's file,
  and it sits at the top of the gaps. That is the honest place
  for it.
- The innocent reading always rides along: a partial plate is
  not a plate; a lyric is not a confession; a repost is not a
  witness.

## 3. Three new planted-catch patterns (for a third case, or Screens v2)

Each one is a contradiction the squad can catch with two quotes,
in the house style. None needs a new agent.

1. **The livestream alibi.** "I was live all night" vs the chat
   replay: the stream went dark 11:02-11:40 PM, and a comment
   at 11:05 says "where'd he go." Gap: the dark 38 minutes.
2. **The background that moves.** Two posts, same night, same
   outfit, different cities: a mural in post A and a shopfront
   in post B, 40 minutes apart, 90 minutes apart by road.
   One of the timestamps is wrong, or one post is old. The
   board flags the travel impossibility, not the person.
3. **The repost chain.** A claim ("he was at the studio") is
   repeated by five accounts — but all five quote the same
   single deleted original. Five citations, one source. The
   board collapses the chain to one and downgrades the claim.

## 4. Judge Q&A, one line each

- "Isn't this just accusing people?" — "No. It judges the file.
  Every finding carries two quotes and an innocent reading."
- "What if the crowd is wrong?" — "Boston, 2013. That is why
  the board exists: the crowd is fast, the board checks."
- "Can it talk to witnesses?" — "No, and it says so. It writes
  the exact question for the human detective to ask."
- "Why two cases?" — "One police file, one internet file. Same
  squad, same rules. The internet is where the witnesses are
  now — in public, timestamped, and deletable."
