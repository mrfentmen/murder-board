• Surveillance video: Footage captured the August 4, 2020 attack outside a luxury store on East Oak Street, where attackers exited two vehicles and opened fire on Weekly.
• Cell phone data: Location and communications data helped connect suspects to the incident and timeline.
• Social media and music: Prosecutors used social media posts and rap/diss tracks to show how O-Block promoted its criminal enterprise and claimed responsibility for violence.
• Witness testimony: Witnesses helped establish O-Block's structure as a racketeering enterprise and tie individuals to the case. **1. Surveillance footage established where the suspects came from.**

On August 4, 2020, FBG Duck was shot outside a Dolce & Gabbana store on Oak Street in Chicago.

Investigators obtained surveillance footage showing several suspects leaving Parkway Gardens (O Block) shortly before the murder.

The footage showed two vehicles:

- A Ford Fusion belonging to Tacarlos Offerd.
- A Chrysler 300 associated with Kenneth Roberson.

**2. Chicago's camera network tracked the vehicles.**

This was one of the most important parts of the investigation.

Investigators used:

- City surveillance cameras.
- Police POD cameras.
- Automated license plate readers.

They reconstructed the vehicles' journeys from Parkway Gardens toward Oak Street. The two cars appeared on multiple cameras traveling together, including within seconds of each other.

This helped establish a connection between the suspects' departure and the shooting.&#x20;

## **What actually solved the case?**

The strongest aspect was the combination of independent evidence:

1. Cameras showed suspects leaving O Block.
2. Cameras tracked their vehicles toward the crime scene.
3. Phone evidence helped corroborate movements.
4. A recovered vehicle contained a cartridge casing ballistically linked to the crime scene.
5. Witnesses and digital evidence helped establish identities, roles and motive.

It wasn't simply a case of police recognizing somebody from a photograph. They built a timeline and connected multiple sources of evidence.

The particularly interesting part is how Chicago's existing camera infrastructure helped reconstruct the movements of two vehicles across the city. That was a major component of the investigation.

You could build a multi-agent criminal investigation system that functions like an autonomous investigative team. Its job would be to analyze evidence, reconstruct events, find connections, identify investigative leads, and prepare evidence-backed case files for human investigators.

The important distinction: agents should autonomously investigate evidence, not autonomously declare someone a murderer or decide to arrest them.

## **1. The architecture**

I would build six specialist agents.

**Central Investigation Coordinator**

Assigns jobs, maintains case state, checks results

**Evidence Agent**

Organizes footage, documents, records and statements.

**Timeline Agent**

Reconstructs events using verified timestamps.

**Connection Agent**

Finds independently supported connections between evidence items.

**Media Agent**

Reviews authorized video and identifies relevant events.

**Contradiction Agent**

Looks for inconsistencies, missing evidence and alternative explanations.

**Case Builder Agent**

Produces investigative reports with source references.

**Human Investigator**

Reviews findings, verifies leads and authorizes investigative actions.

## **2. What information would they investigate?**

Only material obtained through lawful, authorized sources.

| Evidence source    | What agents can do                                |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| CCTV footage       | Identify relevant events and timestamps           |
| Public records     | Organize relevant documented facts                |
| Witness statements | Compare accounts and identify discrepancies       |
| Forensic reports   | Cross-reference documented findings               |
| Phone records      | Analyze lawfully obtained records                 |
| Vehicle records    | Correlate documented vehicle evidence             |
| Digital evidence   | Organize authorized messages and files            |
| Case archives      | Find connections to previously investigated cases |

An important requirement: preserve original evidence, document its source, and maintain a complete chain of custody. An AI-generated observation is not itself proof.

## **3. How an investigation would work**

Imagine a homicide investigation.

1. **A case is opened**

   An investigator provides the case material and authorizes which evidence sources the agents can access.
2. **Evidence is processed**

   Agents organize the files, extract relevant information and index the original sources.
3. **A timeline is constructed**

   The timeline agent organizes documented events and flags conflicting or uncertain timestamps.
4. **Independent analysis begins**

   Each agent examines the evidence from a different perspective.
5. **Agents challenge each other's findings**

   The contradiction agent looks for alternative explanations and evidence that weakens the emerging theory.
6. **An investigative report is produced**

   Findings include source references, confidence, uncertainties and outstanding questions.
7. **A human investigator verifies the leads**

   No automated accusation, arrest, confrontation or escalation.

## **4. The most important part: evidence verification**

I would make this a core system requirement.

Every finding must have:

- An original evidence reference.
- A timestamp or document location.
- An explanation of what was observed.
- Whether it is a fact, inference or hypothesis.
- An uncertainty estimate.
- Alternative explanations.
- Independent corroboration, where available.

For example:

| Finding                               | Status                           |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Vehicle appears in footage            | Observed                         |
| Vehicle appears in another recording  | Independently observed           |
| Both recordings show the same vehicle | Inference requiring verification |
| Vehicle occupant committed murder     | Unproven allegation              |

The agents must never silently convert an inference into a fact.

## **5. How I would implement it using your Meta OS architecture**

Your existing message bus, job queue, capability system, watchdog and memory services provide a reasonable foundation.&#x20;

I would add four services:

1. **Evidence Vault:** immutable originals, cryptographic hashes, provenance and access logs.
2. **Investigation Engine:** distributes evidence-analysis jobs among specialist agents.
3. **Case Graph:** connects evidence items, events and documented relationships without treating connections as proof of guilt.
4. **Investigation Dashboard:** displays timelines, evidence, contradictions, source documents and human review decisions.

Agents should have narrowly scoped permissions. They shouldn't have unrestricted access to police systems, private accounts, or external surveillance infrastructure.

## **6. How to know whether it actually works**

Test it against previously closed cases where the evidentiary record and independently established outcomes are available.

Measure:

- How many important pieces of evidence it finds.
- How often it produces unsupported connections.
- How accurately it reconstructs timelines.
- Whether it catches contradictions investigators missed.
- How often it incorrectly implicates uninvolved people.
- Whether it reduces investigative time.

Use blinded evaluations and independent human reviewers.

**My recommendation:** Build an autonomous investigative intelligence platform, not an autonomous police force.

The genuinely interesting research problem is whether multiple independently operating agents can find connections across large volumes of evidence, challenge their own conclusions, and produce better investigative leads than a conventional single-agent system.

That is a technically meaningful project, and it can be tested against real historical case records without putting real people at risk.
