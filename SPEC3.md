
# EXPANSION 2 — the two front doors (Del, 2026-11-20 evening)

Not everyone arrives with a case file. The product has two doors.

## Door 1 — the full file

A detective, a PI, a unit with a cold case: documents in hand. This is
the pipeline as specced: Intake, Timeline, Cross-Examiner, Sergeant.
Evidence in hand helps the agents enormously; this door is the demo's
main run.

## Door 2 — a name and a photo

A family member, a nonprofit, a journalist. What they have is almost
nothing: the name of a dead person, maybe a photograph, a date of
birth, a state. No file exists yet. The product's job at this door is
to BUILD the starter file.

A fifth agent works this door: **Records**. Given name, DOB, state,
and a photo, it runs a records census:

- Death records and the Social Security Death Index.
- Obituaries and funeral home notices.
- Newspaper archives (the death, the funeral, any coverage at the
  time).
- Census and public records where public (federal census schedules
  are public after 72 years; the 1950 census is the latest open one).
- Court records portals where the state publishes them.
- NamUs and the Doe Network for unidentified and missing persons.

Everything Records brings back is labeled at the door: SOURCED (with
the record it came from) or UNVERIFIED (a possible match the human
must confirm). Name collisions are expected and shown, never silently
merged. The output is a starter board: a person card, a rough
timeline, and a list of what to request next — which is exactly the
moment Door 1 takes over.

## What police use, honestly

Real departments also run credentialed systems the public cannot
touch: NCIC, Thomson Reuters CLEAR, LexisNexis Accurint, TLO. Those
are not in this product and the pitch never claims them. Our alley —
PIs, cold case nonprofits, journalists, families — works the public
record stack, and Door 2 is built for exactly that stack. A
department version plugs the credentialed systems in later, under
their own access rules.

## Demo treatment

Door 2 gets a 30 second beat inside the demo, not its own run: the
Sergeant's brief ends, and Del shows the second door — a name, a
photo, a state — and Records assembling the starter board for the
fictional victim from his planted public footprint (an obituary and
two news items generated at the event with the case documents).
One line: "Some people arrive with a file. Some arrive with a
photograph. The board starts either way."
