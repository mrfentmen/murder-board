# EXPANSION 3 — the cast at work, and the murder book

## The artifact has a real name

Every homicide unit keeps a **murder book**: the binder (now a folder
tree) that holds everything on the case, in order, forever. Detectives
live and die by whether the murder book is complete. This product's
output is not a dashboard. It is the murder book, built and kept
current by the cast. The Board is how you look at it; the Brief is its
first page. Pitch line: "It builds the murder book, and it keeps it
honest."

## How the cast hands work around

The cast runs as a squad, not a row of buttons. Fixed hand-offs:

1. **First Officer** logs everything in. Nothing exists until he
   stamps it.
2. **Records** works Door 2 in parallel; his starter file enters
   through the First Officer like everything else.
3. **The Coroner** moves early: his death window is the frame the
   Analyst builds the clock inside, and every alibi is judged
   against it.
4. **CSI** reconciles the ledger before the Cross-Examiner runs, so
   evidence gaps are facts by the time statements get judged.
5. **The Analyst** builds clock and board from Intake, Coroner, CSI,
   and Digital together.
6. **Digital** feeds machine events into the clock and checks claims
   against records the way the Cross-Examiner checks claims against
   claims.
7. **The Interviewer** reads the Cross-Examiner's flags and turns
   them into the next interviews.
8. **The Prosecutor** reads everything last before the brief: he can
   veto a sentence, downgrade a lead's court rating, and add the
   corroboration list.
9. **The Lead Detective** closes: ranks, assigns, writes the brief
   the Prosecutor has already cut to size.

Conflict rule, stated on stage if asked: when agents disagree, the
record beats the statement, the body beats both, and the brief shows
the fight instead of hiding it.

## On stage: the squad room strip

Ten agents is a cast, not a demo. The screen shows the squad room as
a strip of ten named chairs across the top, each lighting as its
agent works and posting its findings to the board. The demo
spotlights four (Coroner sets the window, CSI finds the casing,
Cross-Examiner lands the car, Lead Detective writes the brief). The
other six are visibly present and working, because a real case is a
squad, and the Managed Agents story is the size of the cast.

Demo script addition (slots into the 1:10 beat): "A homicide case
is not one detective. It is a squad: the examiner, the scene tech,
the analyst, the prosecutor reading over your shoulder. So the
board is a squad too. Ten agents, ten real jobs."

## Build consequence (tomorrow)

Agent files are configuration, not code paths: one runner, ten
prompt files from AGENTS.md, each with the same JSON discipline.
Adding the cast costs prompts and screen chairs, not architecture.
If time runs short, the cast cuts from the bottom of the hand-off
list (Digital and Interviewer fold into Analyst and Cross-Examiner)
and the four core agents still carry the demo.
