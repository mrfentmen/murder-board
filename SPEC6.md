# SPEC 6 — how detectives work, and the no-contact doctrine

Written 2026-11-21 at Del's order: research how movie detectives act
and find crimes, put it together with the build, and write down the
bottleneck: **the AI cannot contact people. Contacting people is
what a detective does. So the squad goes by internet clues and
paper.** Research brief: movie detectives and real practice,
researched 2026-11-21 (LAPD Murder Book profile, National Police
Foundation cold case checklist, OJP homicide strategies, PEACE
interviewing, and the screen canon).

## The loop (screen and real detectives run the same one)

1. Collect without theorizing. Holmes: it is a capital mistake to
   theorize before one has data.
2. Put everything in one indexed file, in time order. The real
   murder book is a standardized binder anyone on the squad can
   pick up; ours is the book object, and no fact lives only in a
   chat.
3. Build the timeline, then compare: person against person, person
   against record, statement one against statement three.
4. Notice the small thing: the inconsistency, the absence, the
   explanation that fits too neatly (Blanc's donut hole: a story
   with no hole has another hole inside it).
5. Go back. Re-read the file, re-pull the record that settles it,
   draft the question that cracks it. Columbo's "just one more
   thing" is a final check against the one source nobody consulted.
6. Let the record speak last. If a person articulates an alibi, real
   guidance says verify it immediately against premises, effects,
   and records. Asking is contact. Checking is desk work.

## The doctrine: asking is CONTACT, checking is DESK

The squad never contacts anyone: no canvass, no interview, no
surveillance, no visit, and it never claims it did. Everything the
squad does is DESK work, the half of detective work that runs on
paper and public information:

- the murder book and the chronological record
- timeline reconstruction and statement comparison
- alibi verification against a record (tow log, receipt, timetable,
  camera log), never against a person's word alone
- records pulls and archive trawls: the follow-the-paper trail
  (Freamon on The Wire is the patron saint of this build: quiet,
  methodical, the case breaks in the paperwork)
- the absence audit: what should be in this file that is not
- the gap audit on other people's legwork: was the canvass done,
  was the camera pulled, was the witness re-interviewed
- interview preparation and evaluation (the PEACE half that is
  desk work): the questions, in order, each anchored to a quote

Where a lead needs a person, the lead names the exact question the
human detective asks, or the exact record to pull and what it could
prove or disprove. The board prepares the knock on the door. The
detective knocks.

## Door 2 is where the internet clues live

A name and a photo become a starter file from the public record
stack only: death index, obituaries and funeral notices, newspaper
archives, court portals, census schedules, and the open web. Every
item labeled SOURCED or UNVERIFIED. Name collisions shown, never
merged. This is Graysmith's half of Zodiac: the obsessive collator
with boxes of reports from four jurisdictions, working likelihood
in public paper, and honest that likelihood is not proof.

## How the squad talks (borrowed, sparingly)

- "Let's put it in time order first."
- "The record says. The account says. Those are different things."
- "On this date the account was X. In the record it is Y. That gap
  is the case."
- "What should be here that isn't?"
- "Follow the paperwork: who was paid, who filed, who benefited?"
- "Eliminated, on what? Name the source."
- "One more thing: we haven't checked that record yet."
- "Likelihood, not proof, on this point."

Never: gotcha language, a profile presented as an identification,
or a suppressed record because it fights the lead theory.

## What this changes in the build

- Lead Detective leads: one action each, components earned singly,
  and every lead is either a record pull or a question for the
  human. Done in the prompt, 2026-11-21.
- The Interviewer's questions are explicitly FOR the detective to
  ask in the room. The squad writes them; it does not ask them.
- Records (Door 2) is the growth path after the demo: the census
  over public internet sources, labeled and collision-safe.
