Explainer, August 2020, updated with later reporting. The feud
behind the shooting, as described by police, prosecutors, and
Chicago news reporting.

Carlton Weekly performed as FBG Duck. He was associated with
Tookaville, a faction of the Gangster Disciples around 63rd
Street on the South Side. Tookaville is named for Shondale
"Tooka" Gregory, a teenager killed in 2011.

His rivals were O Block, a set of the Black Disciples based in
and around the Parkway Gardens apartments on the South Side.
O Block is named for Odee Perry, killed in 2011.

For years the two sides traded drill songs aimed at each
other, naming dead members of the other side. Federal
investigators later said the diss tracks between Duck and
O Block artists fueled violence on the streets.

On July 10, 2020, Weekly released a song called "Dead
Bitches." It taunted deceased O Block members by name,
including Odee Perry and T-Roy.

After the murder, an informant told Chicago police and FBI
agents that a bounty had been offered for killing Weekly:
$50,000 at first, later raised to $100,000. Prosecutors later
alleged the bounty came from O Block rapper King Von. The
bounty was an allegation. King Von was never charged in this
case, and he was himself killed in November 2020.
